Let's start with the word everyone uses. The algorithm. People say it like it's a person. The algorithm hates me. The algorithm loves this video. But really, it's not a person at all. It's a recommendation system. A model that looks at what you do, and guesses what you'll want next.

And what does it look at? Pretty much everything. What you watch. How long you watch it. Whether you rewatch it. Whether you pause, or like, or share, or comment. Whether you scroll past in half a second. Even the tiny moment you hesitate before scrolling. All of that is a signal.

Here's the part that surprised me. The most important signal usually isn't the like button. It's time. How long you stay. Because a like is something you choose to do. But how long you stay? That's something you barely notice. And that's exactly why it's so honest.

So think about what that means. The system doesn't really learn what you say you want. It learns what you can't stop looking at. And those two things are not always the same, are they?

Now, I'm not saying this is evil. Honestly, it's kind of brilliant engineering. It's one of the most impressive pieces of technology most people use every day. It's just that brilliant and good for you are not always the same thing.

So how does it actually decide what to show you? Let's keep it simple. Imagine every video in the world sitting in a giant room. Billions of them. The system can't look at all of them for you. So it does it in steps.

Step one. It grabs a few thousand videos that might be relevant. Stuff that people similar to you enjoyed. Stuff from accounts you follow. Stuff that's trending near you. That's the rough shortlist.

Step two. It ranks them. It gives every video a score. How likely are you to watch this? To finish it? To share it? And then it puts the highest scores at the top.

Step three. It mixes things up a little. Because if it only showed you the exact same kind of thing, you'd get bored and leave. So it throws in a few surprises. A new creator. A different topic. Just to test you. And if you bite, well, now it knows something new about you.

And here's the really wild part. It does all of that in a fraction of a second. Every single time you swipe. For billions of people. At the same time. That's the kind of thing that makes me stop and go, okay, that's genuinely incredible.

But let's talk about the side effects, because that's where it gets interesting. When a system rewards whatever keeps people watching, what kind of content wins? Usually the most extreme version. The angriest take. The most shocking clip. The loudest voice in the room.

Not because anyone designed it to spread anger. But because anger works. It grabs attention. It makes people comment. It makes people share. And the system, which only sees the numbers, just learns that this kind of thing performs. So it shows more of it.

So, what can you actually do about all this? Good news. Quite a lot. And none of it requires deleting every app and moving to the mountains.

First. Remember that you're training it. Every time you watch something, you're teaching the system. So if you want a better feed, feed it better. Watch the stuff you actually value, all the way through. Skip the stuff you don't, quickly. Within a few days, you'll notice the difference.

Second. Use the tools that are already there. Most apps have a not interested button. Hardly anyone uses it. Use it. It's one of the clearest signals you can send.

Third. Notice the feeling. If you come off the app feeling worse than when you opened it, that's information. Your brain is telling you something. Listen to it.

Fourth. Set a limit, and make it physical if you have to. Put the phone in another room at night. Use an actual alarm clock. It sounds old fashioned. It works.

And fifth, my favourite. Go looking for things on purpose. Search for a topic. Read a long article. Pick a book. When you choose what you see, instead of letting it be chosen for you, the whole experience changes. You go from passenger to driver.
