## 6. Calm explaining (slow and warm, like teaching a friend)

Let me explain this simply. Imagine a library with every book ever written, and a librarian who has read all of them but can't remember where any single sentence came from.
That librarian is the model. When you ask a question, it doesn't look anything up. It writes an answer that sounds like the books it read.
Most of the time, that works really well. But sometimes it writes something that sounds right and simply isn't true.
That's what people call a hallucination. It's not lying, because it doesn't know what lying is. It's just guessing with confidence.
And that's exactly why we check the source. Every claim, every number, every chart.
Think of it like a recipe. The ingredients are the data, the oven is the computer, and the training is the time it spends cooking.
If the ingredients are bad, it doesn't matter how good the oven is. You'll still get a bad cake.
So the next time you see a big announcement, slow down, breathe, and ask one question: where's the proof?
